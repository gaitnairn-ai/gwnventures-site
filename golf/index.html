01 — The problem with mats

Every simulator in New York puts you on a synthetic mat.

A mat is a forgiving surface pretending to be an honest one. It flatters your contact and hides the information you came to practice for.

01

Fat shots skid instead of digging

On a mat

A heavy strike bounces off the surface and flies anyway. The mat absorbs the mistake for you.

On real grass

The club digs. You feel it in your hands and see it in the ball flight — immediately.

02

Mishits aren't punished honestly

On a mat

Contact a course would expose gets quietly forgiven, so your numbers read better than your swing.

On real grass

Turf responds the way the course will. What you practice is what you'll actually shoot.

03

There's no divot to read

On a mat

Nothing is left behind. The single richest piece of feedback in golf simply doesn't exist.

On real grass

Depth, direction and start point of every divot tell you exactly what the club did.

04

Club-to-turf interaction is wrong

On a mat

How the sole enters and exits synthetic fibre doesn't match grass. You're grooving a different move.

On real grass

Practise the lie you'll actually play, at the surface you'll actually play it from.

02 — Real grass, indoors

How it works

Grass doesn't have to survive indoors. It only has to arrive fresh.

01 — Grown outside

Cultivated off-site, under real conditions

Our turf is grown by greenhouse and nursery partners in natural light and open air — the same way the grass on a course grows. Nothing is asked to live in a windowless room.

02 — Rotated in

Fresh turf, brought into the bays

Living grass is delivered and rotated through the hitting bays on a schedule, so every session is played off healthy turf. Used sections go back out to recover and regrow.

03 — Playable year-round

Climate-controlled, every month

Real-turf practice in the middle of a New York winter, without a 60–90 minute drive out of the city in each direction. The season stops being a thing you plan around.
